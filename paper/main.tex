\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is widely used to ground language-model answers in statutory documents, yet sentence-level groundedness and document-level retrieval recall do not verify whether an answer cites the governing legal article and clause or abstains when the corpus lacks a covering rule. To measure this gap on Vietnamese banking regulation, we introduce \textbf{RegRAG-VN}, a provenance-guarded benchmark of 22 official regulatory instruments and 88 curated items split into answerable statutory questions and unanswerable probes across a two-tier corpus separating generation fixtures from full-corpus retrieval. Across 792 runs of three open-weight 3B--7B models (Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-3B, and Vistral-7B) under closed-book, BM25, and BGE-M3 dense retrieval, high sentence groundedness (0.912--0.985) and document recall at $k{=}3$ (90.6\% Doc@3) still accompany low article citation F1 (0.434), low clause accuracy (0.317), and weak probe abstention (0.000--0.500).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pairs an external document index with a generative model to ground outputs in retrieved evidence~\cite{lewis2020rag,gao2024ragsurvey} and reduce hallucination~\cite{ji2023hallucination}. In legal domains, however, empirical audits of retrieval-backed AI research tools still document persistent citation and grounding errors~\cite{magesh2025hallucinationfree}. Prior work on faithfulness and attribution has also shown that lexical overlap with source text does not guarantee that generated claims are supported by the cited passage~\cite{maynez2020faithfulness,rashkin2023attribution,bohnet2022attributed,min2023factscore}, and that chunking granularity~\cite{qin2025mixgranularity} and context position~\cite{liu2024lostmiddle} affect how models use retrieved passages.

Existing benchmarks evaluate general-domain RAG robustness~\cite{liu2024rgb,es2024ragas}, English legal retrieval and end-to-end legal RAG~\cite{pipitone2024legalbenchrag,butler2026legalragbench}, and Vietnamese legal question answering, reasoning, or statutory retrieval~\cite{nguyen2025vlqa,dong2025vlegalbench,nguyen2026vilegallm,nguyen2024vnlegalir}. To our knowledge, no existing benchmark jointly audits exact article (\emph{Điều}) and clause (\emph{Khoản}) citation faithfulness alongside unanswerable-probe abstention on Vietnamese banking regulations for compact open-weight 3B--7B models~\cite{yang2024qwen25,nguyen2023vistral}.

We present \textbf{RegRAG-VN}, a provenance-guarded benchmark of official Vietnamese banking regulations that pairs answerable statutory questions with unanswerable probes across a two-tier corpus separating gold-passage generation fixtures from full-corpus retrieval. Using RegRAG-VN, we evaluate Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct~\cite{yang2024qwen25}, and Vistral-7B-Chat~\cite{nguyen2023vistral} under closed-book, BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25,rankbm25}, and BGE-M3 dense retrieval~\cite{chen2024m3} to answer three research questions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1 (Hallucination and groundedness):} How much does retrieval reduce hallucination compared with closed-book generation, and does sentence-level groundedness reflect citation faithfulness?
\item \textbf{RQ2 (Citation faithfulness and abstention):} How accurately do compact models cite exact articles (\emph{Điều}) and clauses (\emph{Khoản}), and how reliably do they abstain on unanswerable probes?
\item \textbf{RQ3 (Retrieval bottleneck):} On the full statutory corpus, how does instrument-level recall compare with article-level recall?
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{RAG systems and evaluation.} Retrieval-augmented generation pairs a document index with a generative model~\cite{lewis2020rag,gao2024ragsurvey} using sparse lexical rankers such as BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25,rankbm25} or dense neural retrievers~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr,chen2024m3}. General-domain RAG evaluation suites measure robustness to noise and unanswerable inputs~\cite{liu2024rgb} or automate faithfulness and relevance scoring~\cite{es2024ragas}. In the legal domain, LegalBench-RAG~\cite{pipitone2024legalbenchrag} evaluates span-level retrieval on English legal documents, and Legal RAG Bench~\cite{butler2026legalragbench} evaluates end-to-end English legal RAG. Neither benchmark evaluates Vietnamese statutory hierarchy (\emph{Điều}/\emph{Khoản}) citation faithfulness or unanswerable banking probes.

\textbf{Hallucination, attribution, and context length.} Surveys of hallucination in natural language generation~\cite{ji2023hallucination} and empirical analyses of abstractive summarization~\cite{maynez2020faithfulness} show that lexical overlap metrics do not reliably measure factual faithfulness. Attribution frameworks~\cite{rashkin2023attribution,bohnet2022attributed,min2023factscore} therefore evaluate whether generated claims are supported by supplied evidence. In legal NLP, LexGLUE~\cite{chalkidis2022lexglue} benchmarks English legal language understanding, while empirical audits of retrieval-backed legal AI tools~\cite{magesh2025hallucinationfree} find persistent citation and grounding failures. Prior work also demonstrates that chunking granularity~\cite{qin2025mixgranularity} and the position of relevant text within long prompts~\cite{liu2024lostmiddle} affect retrieval and generation accuracy.

\textbf{Vietnamese legal NLP and word segmentation.} Recent resources advance Vietnamese legal question answering~\cite{nguyen2025vlqa}, legal reasoning~\cite{dong2025vlegalbench}, domain-adapted Vietnamese legal LLMs~\cite{nguyen2026vilegallm}, and Vietnamese legal information retrieval~\cite{nguyen2024vnlegalir}. Sparse retrieval over Vietnamese text also relies on word-segmentation toolkits~\cite{vu2018vncorenlp,pyvi}. RegRAG-VN complements these resources by combining a provenance-guarded two-tier banking corpus, exact \emph{Điều}/\emph{Khoản} citation evaluation, unanswerable refusal probes, and human scorer calibration~\cite{cohen1960kappa}.

\subsection{Domain and corpus construction}
We collected 22 Vietnamese banking regulatory instruments from official Vietnamese government and State Bank of Vietnam public portals, spanning National Assembly laws (\emph{Luật}), Government decrees (\emph{Nghị~định}), State Bank circulars (\emph{Thông~tư}), ordinances (\emph{Pháp~lệnh}), and official letters (\emph{Công~văn}), each pinned by its official URL and SHA-1 hash. Across these 22 instruments, statutory rules are organized into nested article (\emph{Điều}) and clause (\emph{Khoản}) hierarchies covering payment accounts, collateral, foreign loans, and capital adequacy.

An automated ingestion coverage gate checks whether each answerable gold passage matches or contains an indexed corpus chunk ($\ge 30$ characters). Exact verbatim coverage reaches 63/64 (98.44\%); the sole exception is \texttt{Q036}, which cites an unnumbered regulatory annex rather than a numbered article, so all article-level (\emph{Điều}) metrics use $n{=}63$.

\subsection{Corpus tiers and the coverage gate}
Evaluating models only on gold passages hides retrieval errors, whereas mixing full-corpus noise into generation confounds retrieval misses with generation unfaithfulness. Our two-tier split separates the two: Tier~1 holds the 64 curated gold passages across 16 document IDs (20 canonical gold document identifiers, including \texttt{Q065}) to isolate generation, while Tier~2 indexes all 22 instruments (3,703 chunks, deduplicated by chunk ID) to audit sparse and dense retrieval recall independently (Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}).

\subsection{Annotation scope}
An expert reviewer audited a stratified 50-output sample (41 answerable, 9 probes) against the official banking statutes to calibrate the automated scorer (Section~\ref{sec:method}), labeling an output faithful only when the cited provision supports its claims and unfaithful when it contradicts or fabricates the statute. A probe is non-covering when it asks about a rule not addressed in the 22 instruments---for example, \texttt{Q037} asks about capital adequacy ratio (CAR) reporting frequency, which is absent from Circular~41/2016/TT-NHNN.

In total, RegRAG-VN comprises 88 items (Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark}): 64 answerable questions (40 with clause-level \emph{Khoản} gold labels) and 24 unanswerable probes. The 24 probes cover payment accounts (\texttt{Q067--Q080}), collateral (\texttt{Q081--Q088}: 7 marital-property and guardianship probes plus 1 parcel-merging probe, \texttt{Q087}), foreign loans (\texttt{Q066}), and CAR reporting frequency (\texttt{Q037}). For all 24 probes, the gold response is ``not in the document repository'' (\emph{Không có trong kho văn~bản}).

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:method}

Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows how the pipeline runs in four steps: the curated gold files, the ingestion and coverage check, retrieval and generation, and the final scoring pass.

\subsection{Retrieval backends, context packing, and provenance}
Because statutory articles frequently exceed prompt budgets, the retrieval stage enforces deterministic top-$k{=}3$ context packing. Each indexed chunk carries document and article header metadata, and retrieval applies strict length bounds to supplied text rather than letting prompts lean on parametric memory alone.

Article precision and recall are measured using exact matches, false positives, and missed citations. A true positive matches a gold article (requiring the right instrument whenever an instrument is named, so a wrong named instrument scores zero); a false positive is a predicted citation matching no gold provision; and a missed citation is an unmatched gold article.

To enforce provenance across all runs, our evaluation pipeline records the corpus source, retriever backend, and metric status on every output row, keeps Tier~1 generation fixtures separated from Tier~2 retrieval indices, and deduplicates chunk IDs before indexing (Section~\ref{sec:disclosure}).

\subsection{Models, prompts, metrics, and human calibration}
For sparse search we tokenize Vietnamese text with PyVi~\cite{pyvi} and rank chunks with \texttt{rank\_bm25}~\cite{robertson2009bm25,rankbm25}. For dense search we use \texttt{BAAI/bge-m3}~\cite{chen2024m3}. In both cases we take the top $k{=}3$ chunks and pack them into at most 7,000 characters, with each chunk capped at 3,500 characters and kept above 400 characters when possible. We test three open-weight models---Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct~\cite{yang2024qwen25}, and Vistral-7B-Chat~\cite{nguyen2023vistral}---in 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) quantization on one RTX~3090 using greedy decoding (\texttt{temperature=0.0}, 512 new tokens max, \texttt{seed=1234}). That gives 792 runs in total: 709 terminate normally and 83 hit the 512-token limit. The prompt tells each model to answer only from the supplied text, cite the exact document, article, and clause, and write ``information not found in the document'' (\texttt{THÔNG~TIN KHÔNG~CÓ TRONG TÀI~LIỆU}) when the text does not answer the question. The core refusal string (\texttt{KHÔNG~CÓ TRONG TÀI~LIỆU}) appears in 320 of the 792 outputs (306 match the full phrase), but 302 of those outputs still provide an answer.

Our automated scorer computes hallucination, sentence groundedness, and probe refusal (Table~\ref{tab:main}), pooled \emph{Điều} F1 across 189 rows (63 questions times 3 models, where citing the right article under the wrong document scores zero) and \emph{Khoản} accuracy across 120 rows (Table~\ref{tab:citation}), as well as Tier~2 recall (Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}). Against the 50-row human audit, Cohen's $\kappa$~\cite{cohen1960kappa} is 0.615 for refusal, 0.386 for hallucination, and 0.204 for correctness (recall is 0.167, missing 15 of 18 expert-approved paraphrases). Citation F1 and retrieval recall match statutory IDs directly and are unaffected by paraphrasing.

\subsection{Quantitative results across RQ1, RQ2, and RQ3}
Table~\ref{tab:main} and Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(a) show how often each model hallucinates. Dense search cuts hallucination on Qwen2.5-7B from 0.438 down to 0.313 (a 28.6\% relative drop) and on Qwen2.5-3B from 0.750 down to 0.422. Vistral-7B behaves differently: BM25 pushes its hallucination up from 0.625 to 0.672 (with 22 wrong-document errors and 34 cases where the cited article does not cover the question), and dense search only brings it to 0.594. Yet sentence groundedness stays high across every model, between 0.912 and 0.985 overall (and 0.912 to 0.939 on Vistral-7B). In Table~\ref{tab:citation}, dense \emph{Điều} F1 is 0.452 on Qwen2.5-7B, 0.430 on Vistral-7B, and 0.418 on Qwen2.5-3B. For \emph{Khoản} accuracy, Vistral-7B falls behind at 0.250 (dense) and 0.200 (BM25), while Qwen2.5-7B gets 0.350 and 0.325 and Qwen2.5-3B gets 0.350 and 0.400. A Pearson $\chi^2$ test comparing runs where the gold passage is intact in the prompt against runs where it is missing gives $\chi^2{=}10.49$ for BM25 and $\chi^2{=}13.63$ for dense ($df{=}1$), so having the right passage helps, yet 33.3\% (BM25) and 34.5\% (dense) of intact runs still get the citation wrong.

\subsection{Diagnostic analysis, corpus hierarchy, and limitations}
\label{sec:disclosure}
Comparing Tables~\ref{tab:main} and~\ref{tab:citation} separates broad generation reliability from citation-level faithfulness: retrieval reduces hallucination and sustains high sentence groundedness, yet groundedness does not verify whether the cited statute, article, and clause are valid. Regulatory auditing therefore requires exact provision-level citation checks rather than surface overlap alone.

Flattening statutory structure into plain-text chunks allows standard sparse and dense retrievers to operate directly, but on RegRAG-VN this causes a sharp drop from instrument-level recall to article recall and citation accuracy (Tables~\ref{tab:citation}--\ref{tab:retrieval}). Whether hierarchy-aware indexing recovers article-level accuracy remains open for future evaluation.

Three scope boundaries apply to our findings. First, the 512-token generation cap can clip trailing citations on verbose outputs (Section~\ref{sec:method}), and we evaluated only 4-bit NF4 3B--7B models (larger or full-precision checkpoints remain untested). Second, generation was evaluated on Tier~1 gold-passage fixtures (an upper bound on retrieval), while Tier~2 retrieval was audited separately. Third, automated lexical correctness under-estimates expert-approved legal paraphrases (Section~\ref{sec:method}), although abstention agreement is substantial and article/clause citation scoring is deterministic.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced \textbf{RegRAG-VN}, a provenance-guarded benchmark for Vietnamese banking regulation, and audited three compact open-weight models across closed-book, BM25, and dense RAG modes. Our experiments show that high sentence-level groundedness and strong document-level recall still mask substantial article- and clause-level citation errors as well as weak abstention on unanswerable probes---even when the gold passage appears intact in the prompt. Surface groundedness and document recall are therefore insufficient proxies for statutory citation faithfulness, motivating future work on hierarchy-aware retrieval, provenance-checked citation verification, and calibrated abstention.
