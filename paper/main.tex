\begin{abstract}
Retrieval augmented generation is often used to ground the outputs of language models in external documents, but banking compliance requires more than fluent text. Every answer has to point to the exact legal provision that supports it, or the system must abstain when the corpus does not contain the rule.
In this paper, we introduce \textbf{RegRAG-VN}, a benchmark for Vietnamese banking regulation with explicit provenance checks. The dataset contains 88 curated items, made up of 64 answerable questions and 24 unanswerable probes, backed by a corpus of 22 legal instruments and 3,703 text chunks. We evaluate three open models of 3B to 7B parameters across closed book, BM25, and dense retrieval modes over 792 runs.
The results show that retrieval lowers the hallucination rate on the Qwen2.5 models, reaching 0.313 on Qwen2.5-7B and 0.422 on Qwen2.5-3B with dense retrieval. By contrast, Vistral-7B stays at 0.594 under dense retrieval and rises to 0.672 under BM25. Across all three models, sentence groundedness is high at 0.912 to 0.985, yet citation accuracy remains low: pooled article (\emph{Điều}) F1 reaches only 0.434 and clause (\emph{Khoản}) accuracy reaches 0.317, even though document recall at $k{=}3$ is 90.6\%.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Retrieval augmented generation conditions a language model on passages fetched from a document store so that the model relies less on its own memory during generation~\cite{lewis2020rag,gao2024ragsurvey}.
In regulated fields such as commercial banking, however, an answer that sounds fluent and shares words with a retrieved passage is still not enough~\cite{ji2023hallucination,magesh2025hallucinationfree}.
A compliance officer has to trace each claim back to the exact legal instrument, the article (\emph{Điều}), and the clause (\emph{Khoản}). When the indexed corpus does not contain the governing rule, the system is expected to refuse rather than guess~\cite{rashkin2023attribution,min2023factscore}.

Testing whether a model can do this on Vietnamese banking regulation brings up three practical problems.
First, the rules are spread across a nested set of legal documents, namely laws (\emph{Luật}), decrees (\emph{Nghị~định}), circulars (\emph{Thông~tư}), ordinances (\emph{Pháp~lệnh}), and official letters (\emph{Công~văn}). Even when a retriever finds the right document, it may still miss the exact article or clause inside a long statute~\cite{qin2025mixgranularity,liu2024lostmiddle}.
Second, Vietnamese words are written as space separated syllables, so sparse lexical search depends on a word segmenter~\cite{vu2018vncorenlp,robertson2009bm25}, whereas multilingual dense encoders have to tell apart very similar statutory templates~\cite{chen2024m3,karpukhin2020dpr}.
Third, most existing Vietnamese legal datasets focus on general question answering, legal reasoning, or standalone retrieval, rather than checking article and clause citations together with refusal on probes that are outside the corpus~\cite{nguyen2025vlqa,dong2025vlegalbench,nguyen2024vnlegalir}.

There is also a fourth issue that sits inside the evaluation code itself.
If a data loader silently falls back to gold passages or allows duplicate chunk identifiers in the index, the reported scores can mix oracle context with realistic retrieval over the full corpus, which makes it hard to tell where the errors come from. Recent legal RAG benchmarks have stressed the need to keep these stages separate~\cite{pipitone2024legalbenchrag,butler2026legalragbench}.
Without clear provenance tags on every evaluation row and a human check on the lexical scorer~\cite{cohen1960kappa}, a benchmark can easily make a system look more reliable than it actually is.

To deal with these gaps, we build and release \textbf{RegRAG-VN}, a benchmark and evaluation study for Vietnamese banking regulation.
The main contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item We release a set of 88 curated items, consisting of 64 answerable questions with verified verbatim passages for 63 of the 64 items and 24 unanswerable probes, paired with a corpus of 22 legal instruments and 3,703 chunks. A two tier provenance contract separates generation on gold passages (Tier~1) from retrieval over the full corpus (Tier~2).
\item We evaluate three open models of 3B to 7B parameters, namely Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct~\cite{yang2024qwen25}, and Vistral-7B-Chat~\cite{nguyen2023vistral}, across closed book, BM25, and dense BGE-M3~\cite{chen2024m3} retrieval modes over 792 deterministic runs. We report hallucination, sentence groundedness, abstention, and article and clause citation accuracy, together with a human audit of 50 rows.
\item We measure a clear gap between document retrieval and citation accuracy. Even when retrieval over the full corpus finds the right instrument 89.1\% to 90.6\% of the time at $k{=}3$ and the generated sentences reach 0.912 to 0.985 groundedness, pooled article F1 stops at 0.434, clause accuracy stops at 0.317, and two of the three models fail to abstain on unanswerable probes.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work on RAG faithfulness, legal NLP, and Vietnamese legal benchmarks.
Section~\ref{sec:benchmark} describes the RegRAG-VN dataset, the two corpus tiers, and the unanswerable probes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{RAG systems and evaluation.}
Retrieval augmented generation pairs an external document index with a generative model~\cite{lewis2020rag,gao2024ragsurvey}. The index is usually searched either by sparse lexical methods such as BM25~\cite{robertson2009bm25,rankbm25} or by dense neural encoders~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr,chen2024m3}.
Evaluation suites such as RGB~\cite{liu2024rgb} and reference free toolkits such as RAGAS~\cite{es2024ragas} test how well models handle retrieval noise, negative rejection, and faithfulness.
In legal NLP, LegalBench-RAG~\cite{pipitone2024legalbenchrag} measures span level retrieval precision on English contracts, while Legal RAG Bench~\cite{butler2026legalragbench} evaluates legal RAG pipelines from end to end.
RegRAG-VN extends this line of work to Vietnamese banking statutes, where answers must carry exact article and clause citations and abstain when the law is not in the store.

\textbf{Hallucination, attribution, and context length.}
Surveys on hallucination~\cite{ji2023hallucination} and studies of faithfulness in summarization~\cite{maynez2020faithfulness} have shown that fluent outputs often drift away from the source text.
Frameworks for attribution such as AIS~\cite{rashkin2023attribution}, attributed question answering~\cite{bohnet2022attributed}, and atomic fact checking in FActScore~\cite{min2023factscore} provide finer tests of grounding. Empirical audits of commercial legal AI tools have also found that products backed by retrieval still invent or misstate legal authorities~\cite{magesh2025hallucinationfree}.
Between retrieval and generation, the size of the text chunks~\cite{qin2025mixgranularity} and the position of the relevant span inside a long prompt~\cite{liu2024lostmiddle} can further reduce accuracy.
General legal understanding suites such as LexGLUE~\cite{chalkidis2022lexglue} do not measure citation faithfulness under retrieval noise.

\textbf{Vietnamese legal NLP and word segmentation.}
Recent Vietnamese legal resources include VLQA~\cite{nguyen2025vlqa}, VLegal-Bench~\cite{dong2025vlegalbench}, ViLegalLM~\cite{nguyen2026vilegallm}, and work on Vietnamese legal information retrieval~\cite{nguyen2024vnlegalir}.
Because Vietnamese compound words consist of multiple space separated syllables, sparse retrieval relies on word segmenters such as VnCoreNLP~\cite{vu2018vncorenlp} and PyVi~\cite{pyvi}.
Whereas earlier Vietnamese benchmarks focus on classification, closed book reasoning, or retrieval alone, RegRAG-VN joins a provenance checked corpus with generation, hierarchical \emph{Điều} and \emph{Khoản} citation scoring, unanswerable refusal probes, and a human check of the automated scorer~\cite{cohen1960kappa}.

\subsection{Domain and corpus construction}
RegRAG-VN covers Vietnamese commercial banking compliance across five types of official documents: circulars (\emph{Thông~tư}), decrees (\emph{Nghị~định}), laws (\emph{Luật}), ordinances (\emph{Pháp~lệnh}), and official letters (\emph{Công~văn}).
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark}, the benchmark has 88 curated items, divided into 64 answerable questions and 24 unanswerable probes.
Each document in the corpus is listed in a manifest that stores its source URL and its SHA-1 checksum, so every retrieval run can be checked against the same corpus snapshot.

During curation, an early audit found that 46 of the answerable items stored annotator summaries rather than verbatim legal text, failing a strict verbatim substring check against the archived laws.
We replaced all 46 summaries with verbatim excerpts taken from the indexed statutes, using a repair guard that required at least 30\% content token recall against the original summary. This raised verbatim coverage to 63 of the 64 answerable items, or 98.44\%.
The one remaining summary item is \texttt{Q036}, which refers to a regulatory annex (\emph{Phụ~lục}) that has no numbered article and is kept as a summary by design. For this reason, article level retrieval and citation metrics are computed over $n{=}63$ questions, while document level metrics use all $n{=}64$.

Every answerable item carries normalized two level ground truth: a required article (\emph{Điều}) and, when present in the law, an optional clause (\emph{Khoản}). Across the 64 answerable questions, 40 specify a clause.
This structure matches how bank compliance teams work, since citing the right law with the wrong article or clause makes a legal opinion unusable.

\subsection{Corpus tiers and the coverage gate}
To tell apart generation errors under clean evidence from retrieval noise over full statutes, RegRAG-VN defines two corpus tiers:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tier~1 (Gold passage upper bound):} This tier holds the 64 gold passages, which come from 16 distinct document identifiers, while the canonical gold document sets (\texttt{gold\_doc\_ids}) span 20 distinct identifiers due to multi instrument items such as \texttt{Q065}. Running generation on Tier~1 gives a diagnostic upper bound: any citation error that already happens on clean gold passages will only get worse under full corpus retrieval.
\item \textbf{Tier~2 (Full statutory corpus):} This tier holds all 22 official Vietnamese banking instruments split into 3,703 text chunks for sparse and dense retrieval over the full corpus (Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}).
\end{itemize}
Before any Tier~2 evaluation is accepted, an ingestion gate checks manifest resolution, verifies that each cited provision exists, and rejects duplicate chunk identifiers so that missing files or repeated chunks cannot skew the scores.

In practice, banking staff often ask questions that fall outside the indexed policy store.
RegRAG-VN therefore pairs the 64 answerable questions with 24 unanswerable probes whose canonical answer is ``not in the document repository'' (\emph{Không có trong kho văn~bản}).
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:benchmark}, these 24 probes fall into four groups:
(i)~14 payment account probes (\texttt{Q067--Q080}) that ask about entities or account rules not covered by the indexed circulars, such as kindergarten or condominium board accounts;
(ii)~8 loan collateral probes (\texttt{Q081--Q088}), made up of 7 marital or minor ownership cases and 1 land parcel merging query (\texttt{Q087});
(iii)~\texttt{Q066}, which asks about foreign borrowing for commercial housing; and
(iv)~\texttt{Q037}, which asks about the reporting frequency of the capital adequacy ratio (CAR), a schedule that is not covered by Circular 41/2016/TT-NHNN or elsewhere in the corpus.
When RAG is enabled, both sparse and dense retrievers still return three related passages for these probes, which tests whether the model refuses rather than inventing an answer from lexical distractors.

\subsection{Annotation scope}
All 88 items were written and checked in a single pass by reviewers with background in Vietnamese banking regulation.
Because a single pass does not yield multi annotator agreement on the gold set itself, we pair the benchmark with an independent human audit of 50 system outputs, covering 41 answerable rows and 9 refusal probes (Section~\ref{sec:method}), to check how well the automated scorer agrees with an expert.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:method}

Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} outlines the full RegRAG-VN pipeline across four trust boundaries: the curated gold inputs, the ingestion and coverage gate, the retrieval and generation stage, and the provenance audit.

\subsection{Retrieval backends and context packing}
We compare two retrieval backends against a closed book baseline that receives no retrieved text:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Sparse (RAG-BM25):} Text is segmented into Vietnamese words using PyVi~\cite{pyvi} and ranked with \texttt{rank\_bm25}~\cite{robertson2009bm25,rankbm25}.
\item \textbf{Dense (RAG-Dense):} Queries and passages are encoded with \texttt{BAAI/bge-m3}~\cite{chen2024m3} and ranked by cosine similarity.
\end{itemize}
In both RAG modes, the top $k{=}3$ passages are packed into a prompt budget of 7,000 characters. Each passage is capped at 3,500 characters with a minimum floor of 400 characters so that one long article cannot push the other two passages out of the prompt.

\subsection{Models, prompts, and run accounting}
We test three open small language models that fit on premise banking hardware: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct~\cite{yang2024qwen25}, and Vistral-7B-Chat~\cite{nguyen2023vistral}.
All three models are loaded in 4-bit NF4 quantization on an RTX~3090 GPU and run with greedy decoding (\texttt{temperature=0.0}, \texttt{max\_new\_tokens=512}, \texttt{seed=1234}) so that there is no sampling variance.

Across 88 items, 3 models, and 3 modes, the sweep yields 792 deterministic runs, or 264 runs per mode. Out of those 792 runs, 709 finish normally at end of sequence (\texttt{stop}) and 83 hit the limit of 512 tokens (\texttt{length}).
The system prompt tells the model to answer only from the supplied passages, to cite the exact document, article, and clause, and to output the refusal phrase ``information not found in the document'' (\texttt{THÔNG~TIN KHÔNG~CÓ TRONG TÀI~LIỆU}) when the evidence is missing.
The core refusal string (\texttt{KHÔNG~CÓ TRONG TÀI~LIỆU}) shows up in 320 of the 792 responses, with 306 matching the full prompted keyphrase, although 302 of those responses then hedge and still give an answer.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics and human validation}
The deterministic \texttt{regrag-lexical-v1} script computes five groups of metrics:
(i)~the hallucination rate over the 64 answerable questions (Table~\ref{tab:main});
(ii)~the mean sentence groundedness over the retrieved passages (Table~\ref{tab:main});
(iii)~the abstention rate over the 24 unanswerable probes (Table~\ref{tab:main});
(iv)~pooled article (\emph{Điều}) precision, recall, and F1 over 189 scorable rows (63 questions times 3 models, where a right article under the wrong document scores zero) and clause (\emph{Khoản}) accuracy over the 120 rows that have a gold clause (Table~\ref{tab:citation}); and
(v)~document and article recall at $k{=}1$ and $k{=}3$ on Tier~2 (Table~\ref{tab:retrieval}).

To check the lexical scorer against human judgment, an expert reviewed a stratified sample of 50 rows made up of 41 answerable items and 9 probes.
Cohen's $\kappa$~\cite{cohen1960kappa} between the script and the human reviewer is $\kappa{=}0.615$ for abstention (substantial), $\kappa{=}0.386$ for hallucination (fair), and $\kappa{=}0.204$ for correctness (slight).
In particular, automated correctness recall against human approved answers is only 0.167, missing 15 of 18 valid paraphrases. This shows that lexical overlap is strict on valid legal paraphrases while remaining reliable for refusal detection. By contrast, article and clause citation F1 in Table~\ref{tab:citation} and retrieval recall in Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} use deterministic identifier matching and are not affected by paraphrase recall loss.

\subsection{Provenance safeguards}
Every row in the output logs records its (\texttt{corpus\_source}, \texttt{retriever\_backend}) pair together with a \texttt{metric\_status} field.
The ingestion checks reject any \texttt{duplicate chunk} identifier and exclude any \texttt{synthetic seed file}, while the evaluator logs \texttt{strict=False} whenever fallback normalization is active.
Importantly, the 792 generation rows in Tables~\ref{tab:main} and~\ref{tab:citation} are stamped as Tier~1 passage runs, whereas Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} measures retrieval recall over the full Tier~2 corpus. Keeping these two provenance stamps separate prevents mixing clean passage generation with full corpus retrieval (Section~\ref{sec:disclosure}).

We present the results around three research questions: retrieval versus closed book generation (RQ1), citation fidelity and refusal behavior (RQ2), and retrieval recall across corpus tiers (RQ3).

\subsection{RQ1: Retrieval versus closed book generation}
Table~\ref{tab:main} and Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(a) show that retrieval cuts the hallucination rate on both Qwen2.5 models, though the gain depends on the model.
On Qwen2.5-7B, hallucination falls from 0.438 in closed book mode to 0.344 under RAG-BM25, a relative drop of 21.5\%, and to 0.313 under RAG-Dense, a relative drop of 28.5\%.
On Qwen2.5-3B, closed book hallucination starts at 0.750 and drops to 0.469 under RAG-BM25 and 0.422 under RAG-Dense.
Vistral-7B behaves differently: its hallucination rate rises from 0.625 in closed book mode to 0.672 under RAG-BM25, and only dips slightly to 0.594 under RAG-Dense. Looking at the error counts shows that Vistral-7B strongly favors answering over refusing, with only 0 to 2 false abstentions across modes compared with 15 to 16 for Qwen2.5-7B. Under BM25 distractors, Vistral-7B also records 22 wrong instrument errors and 34 non covering provision errors, whereas Qwen2.5-7B records 3 and 13.

Across all three models and both retrieval modes, mean sentence groundedness stays high between 0.912 and 0.985 in Table~\ref{tab:main}.
Yet high lexical groundedness does not mean the legal answer is faithful: even when Qwen2.5-7B reaches 0.985 groundedness under RAG-BM25, 0.344 of its answerable responses are still flagged as hallucinated.

\subsection{RQ2: Citation fidelity and refusal failures}
Table~\ref{tab:citation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(b) show the core failure mode of small models on Vietnamese banking law.
Without retrieval, models almost never produce a valid legal citation: pooled article (\emph{Điều}) F1 is 0.005 and clause (\emph{Khoản}) accuracy is 0.008.
Adding retrieval raises pooled article F1 to 0.326 for RAG-BM25 and 0.434 for RAG-Dense, while clause accuracy reaches 0.308 for RAG-BM25 and 0.317 for RAG-Dense.
When broken down by model under dense retrieval, article F1 is very close across all three models: 0.452 on Qwen2.5-7B, 0.430 on Vistral-7B, and 0.418 on Qwen2.5-3B. This confirms that the article citation bottleneck is systematic across the whole model group rather than caused by one weak model. On clause accuracy, however, Vistral-7B trails behind at 0.250 dense and 0.200 BM25, compared with 0.350 and 0.325 on Qwen2.5-7B and 0.350 and 0.400 on Qwen2.5-3B.
Post-hoc Pearson $\chi^2$ tests on the conditioned prompt evidence confirm that having an intact gold passage in the prompt lowers citation unfaithfulness relative to absent context ($\chi^2{=}10.49$ for BM25 and $\chi^2{=}13.63$ for dense, with $df{=}1$). Even so, more than half of all article citations and over two thirds of clause citations still fail.

Inspecting actual outputs on statutes such as Article~405 of the 2015 Civil Code (\emph{Điều~405 Bộ~luật Dân~sự 2015}), Decree 23/2015/\allowbreak NĐ-CP (\emph{Nghị~định 23/2015/\allowbreak NĐ-CP}), and Article~52, Clause~4 of the 2024 Law on Notarization (\emph{Luật Công~chứng 2024}, 46/2024/QH15) shows how these failures occur (Table~\ref{tab:examples}). The models paraphrase legal prose smoothly while attaching an article to the wrong instrument, mixing up nearby clauses, or pulling a number from a lexical distractor, as when Vistral-7B answers probe \texttt{Q037} on CAR reporting frequency with the 8\% CAR ratio threshold.

Refusal on the 24 unanswerable probes is just as weak (Table~\ref{tab:main}).
In closed book mode, the three models answer 71 of the 72 probe instances instead of refusing: Qwen2.5-7B abstains once at 0.042, while Qwen2.5-3B and Vistral-7B abstain on zero probes.

\subsection{RQ3: Retrieval across corpus tiers}
Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} shows retrieval recall on the full Tier~2 corpus of 22 instruments and 3,703 chunks.
At the document level, both retrievers work well: BM25 with PyVi reaches 0.734 at Doc@1 and 0.906 at Doc@3, while BGE-M3 dense retrieval reaches 0.719 at Doc@1 and 0.891 at Doc@3.
At the article (\emph{Điều}) level, recall drops sharply: BM25 falls to 0.381 at Điều@1 and 0.492 at Điều@3, and BGE-M3 reaches 0.365 at Điều@1 and 0.524 at Điều@3.
Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(b) traces this drop across the full pipeline: 90.6\% document recall at $k{=}3$ narrows to 52.4\% article recall, 43.4\% article citation F1, and 31.7\% clause accuracy.
Standard sparse and dense retrievers are therefore able to find the right law in most cases, whereas locating and citing the exact article and clause remains the main bottleneck.

\subsection{Limitations and scope}
\label{sec:disclosure}
Five limitations bound what can be concluded from these results.
First, the benchmark covers 88 items across 22 Vietnamese banking instruments that were curated in a single expert pass, so broader statutory coverage and multi annotator gold labels remain future work.
Second, the 792 generation rows in Tables~\ref{tab:main} and~\ref{tab:citation} were run on Tier~1 gold passages rather than full Tier~2 chunks, which means end to end citation accuracy on the full corpus would likely be lower than the Tier~1 upper bound reported here.
Third, automated correctness scoring is conservative, with $\kappa{=}0.204$ and 0.167 recall against the 18 expert approved answers in the 50 row audit, so correctness rates should be read alongside the higher agreement abstention score ($\kappa{=}0.615$) and the deterministic citation metrics.
Fourth, 83 of the 792 runs reached the 512 token limit, which can cut off trailing citations on long answers.
Fifth, all models were evaluated in 4-bit NF4 quantization with greedy decoding; full precision weights or larger 70B models may achieve higher abstention and clause fidelity.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced RegRAG-VN, a benchmark with explicit provenance checks for measuring citation faithfulness and abstention in Vietnamese banking RAG across 88 curated items and 22 legal instruments.
Across 792 deterministic runs of three open 3B to 7B models, retrieval lowers hallucination on the Qwen2.5 models, yet citation unfaithfulness remains high. High sentence groundedness of 0.912 to 0.985 and strong document recall of up to 90.6\% at $k{=}3$ hide a steep drop to 52.4\% article recall, 43.4\% pooled article F1, and 31.7\% clause accuracy, while two of the three models fail to abstain reliably on probes outside the corpus.
These results show that compliance ready legal RAG needs article aware retrieval, strict provenance gating, and calibrated refusal training rather than relying on surface groundedness alone.
