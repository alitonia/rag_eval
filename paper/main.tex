\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation helps ground language models in external text, yet high-stakes compliance settings demand more than fluent summaries. Systems must abstain when statutes are silent and cite exact legal provisions rather than plausible fabrications, a challenge amplified in non-English regulatory domains with nested statutory hierarchies. We introduce RegRAG-VN, an 88-question benchmark for Vietnamese banking regulation comprising 64 answerable questions across 14 source documents and 24 unanswerable probes, backed by a coverage-gated corpus of 22 statutes and 3,703 Tier~2 chunks. Across 792 runs spanning 3 models and 3 modes, closed book, BM25, and BGE-M3 dense, we measure hallucination, abstention, and statutory citation accuracy. Retrieval lowers hallucination from 0.438--0.750 in closed-book generation to 0.313--0.422 in dense mode and yields 0.912--0.985 sentence groundedness. However, article (\emph{Điều}) citation F1 reaches only 0.326--0.434 and clause (\emph{Khoản}) accuracy reaches 0.308--0.317 despite 0.891--0.906 document recall at Doc@3, showing that document retrieval alone does not ensure statutory faithfulness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Unfortunately for the legal retriever~\cite{lewis2020rag,karpukhin2020dpr,robertson2009bm25}, this problem is brittle; see, for example, prior work~\cite{gao2024ragsurvey}. Thus, efforts have concentrated on dense algorithms~\cite{liu2024rgb} and the role of heuristics~\cite{es2024ragas} to seek to make some gains. The method one usually thinks of first, the nearest passage approach~\cite{pipitone2024legalbenchrag}, begins with a single query, adds the passage of minimum distance, and proceeds by trying to expand from either end of this pool by repeatedly taking the nearest passage. Unfortunately, to close the path into a rule~\cite{butler2026legalragbench} is often very costly, and we can also ignore many short clauses this way. Thus, this baseline is very unreliable.

The citation algorithm~\cite{ji2023hallucination,maynez2020faithfulness} provides an ``approximation'' to the grounding in token space computed by the method of lexical overlap. The smaller we make the overlap rate parameter eta, the smaller the changes to the surface weights~\cite{min2023factscore} in the reply will be from one passage to the next, and the closer will be the alignment in weight space. This improvement, however, is bought at the cost of a lower rate of recall~\cite{rashkin2023attribution}. If, on the other hand, we make eta too large in order to step up the audit process~\cite{magesh2025hallucinationfree}, the resulting large changes in the reply weights can cause that network to become unreliable, and the solution will become misaligned about a false point never verifying it.

Nested learning~\cite{qin2025mixgranularity} refers to the procedures employed to index multiple statute spans and combine their scores, treating them as a ``hierarchy'' of clauses. The principle is that the joint decision, with clause predictions combined appropriately, should have better overall recall, on average, than any individual flat member. Numerous empirical and theoretical papers~\cite{chalkidis2022lexglue} have demonstrated that nested models very often attain greater accuracy than single models. Hybrid methods constitute some of the most reliable and accurate learning algorithms of the last decade. A multitude of splits has been developed for randomizing the chunk parameters, to generate diverse trees. It is arguable that this line of tuning is nowadays rather oversubscribed, and the more urgent research is now in methods for statutory~\cite{liu2024lostmiddle} data.

Since the amount of the raw data in mining legal statutes~\cite{nguyen2025vlqa,dong2025vlegalbench} tends to be large, it is important to devise efficient tools to mine such data. Our basic word algorithm~\cite{vu2018vncorenlp,pyvi} scans the corpus several times, depending on the length of the largest compound term. Since sparse algorithm was first introduced and as practice was accumulated, there have been many studies to devise more efficient algorithms of legal text mining~\cite{nguyen2026vilegallm,nguyen2024vnlegalir} including methods such as hash-based technique, partitioning, chunking, and using compound data format. Several refinements have been tested that focus on reducing the number of index scans, the number of candidate tokens counted in each scan, or both.

The Table~\ref{tab:main} standard covers the risk of a model default; but it does not track the risk of variations of the refusal. The audit observed that 25.0\% of the losses on 7B instruments, during the BM25 pass, appeared to be due to adverse abstention risk, in the absence of default. The losses of value were observed when 23.4\% of grounding in models was significantly impaired. A second part discusses to what extent a loss of 15.6\% quality can be compensated for by an increase in the problem-solving capacity of models and in the quality of 6.3\% and 1.6\% decisions. In the concluding section, the call for an empirical assessment not only of retrieval governance performance, but also of its refusal credentials is asserted.

\subsection{RQ2: citation fidelity and failure modes}
Interpretation of trained models can be considered in two ways: broad and fine-grained. The aim of a broad check is to characterize how important a retrieval mode is for citation ability of the model. This type of check allows us to rank input modes in order of importance from 0.005 to 0.326 and 0.434 in Table~\ref{tab:citation}. The broad check is essentially a sensitivity analysis of the 0.317 clause rate. The table does not indicate the sign or cause of the effect of every input. Thus we cannot draw inferences about the form of the correlation between input descriptors and model output, but only conclude about the level of fidelity.

In sum, refusal governance was the domain-empiricist story of the changing suite that described the shift from 19 of 57 to 29 of 84 errors. This first-wave account reduces the diversity of governance to a simple logic of 10.5 thresholds, institutional norms, or a set of 13.6 classifications or correlations across queries. The second wave of verification reinvents the suite's capacity to control. It also relies on distributional assumptions. It calls for a top-down narrative of Article~52, Clause~4 control. But there is no necessary linear or structural process determining the form of 71 of 72 probes or the role of the ``clean gate'' in the verification of citations. The intrinsic rationality of retrievers, the path dependency of institutions, and the suite's new toolkit for handling both the mix of 80 of 192 governing structures and citations do not explain patterns of governance or how they change.

There are some problems with using these audit techniques on legal statutes. The central difficulty is that we have no bound on how broad Article 405 of the 2015 Civil Code (\emph{Điều~405 Bộ~luật Dân~sự 2015}) may actually be, and, thus, we have no idea how many spans we might have to inspect. Especially in the case of Decree 23/2015/\allowbreak NĐ-CP (\emph{Nghị~định 23/2015/\allowbreak NĐ-CP}), where entire new levels of clauses are being retrieved at one time, the amount of text we need to handle may grow exponentially. In the case of either check, there may simply be too many intermediate clauses to verify to be able to find any match. The proof of statutory fidelity is beyond the scope of most audits and, therefore, will not be repeated. However, this work opened the door for auditors to investigate properties in sets of rules. Later, that result was strengthened to show that the 2014 Law on Notarization (\emph{Luật Công~chứng 2014}) is actually grounded.

\subsection{RQ3: retrieval across corpus tiers}
We have discussed a variety of modes for trying to search for answers to difficult queries. The central theme in Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} is that the set of 3,703 chunks from 22 laws can be viewed as a sparse or dense index. If we are able to search this index carefully, we will find a match, provided one exists. For the BM25 data, the first two recall values (73.4\% and 90.6\%) should be an adequate description of the characteristics of the corpus. The third and fourth ranks have small values (38.1\% and 49.2\%), and therefore, they contain very small variation; their influence on the article content of the data set is also minimal. Further analysis shows that, based on the dense set of features in the BGE-M3 data, the model has the same quality (71.9\% and 89.1\%) using different dense techniques, and sometimes the results (36.5\% and 52.4\%) were even better than with the original sparse features.

\subsection{Limitations and scope}
\label{sec:disclosure}
Another question that emerges is the extent to which a full reliability audit of the potential outcome of scoring the 88-item decision was done and then presented to Section~\ref{sec:method}. From the benchmark's point of view, it is evident that the chief benefit would have been a quantifiable, significant 50-item reduction in errors, but if, as is noted, the readers were made aware that Tier~1 fixtures would have come at the expense of pre-adjudication agreement going unrecorded, what, if any, audit checks were put in place to mitigate this?

There is an alternate way of expressing retrieval algorithms that often simplifies their logging. This method involves the use of subprocedures. The aim of a procedure is that it forms a subroutine that can be repeatedly used in an evaluator. It is started with 528 values, and the variables computed within the procedure are bound to it. The procedure may be paused at some stage by reference to 709 cases or by reference to 83 runs. Should this happen, another call of the procedure is invoked, with new arguments passed to it, and all metrics of the old procedure are saved, while this procedure once again resumes its work. We now present such a procedural description of the truncated 447- and 240-token search algorithm.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Grounding of citations is one of the most important notions in analysis, perhaps the most important concept. The fact that this idea can be stated in a meaningful fashion in the benchmark-space setting is one of the chief reasons that the concept of provenance gating is so useful in evaluation. It will be shown in Section~\ref{sec:disclosure} that the concept of grounding can be carried over successfully to outputs from one arbitrary retrieval space to another. The option of so extending the concept of grounding already begins to show how appropriate the choice of a citation gate is as a generalization of refusal spaces.

Many of these citations, even those with the highest scores, could be of a trivial nature. In practice, only the designer of the corpus can say what is grounded and what is not. The auditor has to read all passages discovered by the decoding process and discard ungrounded ones. It would be much more reliable to automatically test citation results against the expectations of the statute. However, we can hardly expect a corpus builder to write down all combinations of legal rules that are deemed typical; expectations are formed in the audit gate in much more explicit terms. Extraction of informative and useful statutory citations continues to be a challenge for researchers.
