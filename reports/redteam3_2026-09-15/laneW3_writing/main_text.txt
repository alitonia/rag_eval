Retrieval-augmented generation is well established as a remedy for hallucination in English knowledge-intensive tasks [1],
[2], and simply replicating that aggregate gain in Vietnamese
provides limited practical insight. The failure mode that aggregate accuracy conceals is citation-level unfaithfulness: a model
produces a fluent, plausible answer that carries the wrong
Ðiều or Khoan, or cites an instrument that does not govern
the question at all, instead of abstaining. For a compliance
tool, a wrong answer with a confident, wrong citation is
more dangerous than an obviously wrong answer, because the
citation is what a human reviewer checks first. Measuring that
behaviour requires a benchmark in which the correct citation is
known per question and in which the corpus provably contains
the evidence.
RegRAG-VN is designed around this requirement. Its corpus is QA-driven: the legal instruments ingested are exactly
those cited by the benchmark questions, and a coverage invariant requires every retained question’s verbatim gold passage to
match the ingested text exactly at chunk level. Ground truth is
therefore verifiably present in the retrieval corpus rather than
assumed. On top of this corpus we measure three research

Abstract—Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is the standard remedy for hallucination in small language models, but
its benefit is usually reported as an aggregate accuracy delta
that hides where an answer came from. We present RegRAGVN, a benchmark and evaluation framework for Vietnamese
banking and payment regulation whose retrieval corpus is aligned
with the questions by an exact-match coverage invariant. The
benchmark holds 64 answerable questions drawn from State
Bank of Vietnam circulars, decrees, and laws, alongside 24
unanswerable probes paired with an explicit abstention sentinel.
We evaluate models of at most 7B parameters under closedbook, BM25, and dense retrieval, scoring article-level (Ðiều)
and clause-level (Khoan) citation accuracy alongside abstention.
Across a 3×3×88 generation campaign on a single GPU, retrieval
augmentation on a fixture whose passage pool contains every gold
passage raises article-level citation F1 from 0.005 (closed-book)
to 0.32–0.43. However, 43–49% of RAG answers on answerable
questions still fail citation-level checking—about 35% even when
the governing passage reaches the prompt intact—and models
frequently emit refusal language before answering anyway; a
provenance discipline tags corpus tiers and retriever backends
throughout the pipeline so that degraded or unverified data
cannot silently enter the results.
Index Terms—retrieval-augmented generation, hallucination,
citation faithfulness, abstention, Vietnamese legal NLP, benchmark

Regulatory compliance in Vietnam is a language problem
with legal consequences. A bank officer who answers a customer’s question about payment-card limits, or a compliance
analyst who checks whether a new product violates a circular
of the State Bank of Vietnam (SBV), must locate the governing
provision among hundreds of circulars (Thông tu’), decrees
(Nghı. d̄ı.nh) and laws (Luâ.t), each organised into numbered
articles (Ðiều) and clauses (Khoan). Language models are an
obvious aid, but the models that regional banks and fintechs
can actually deploy are small: a 3–7B parameter model served
on a single commodity GPU, not a frontier API whose data
leaves the country. Whether such models answer Vietnamese
regulatory questions faithfully and cite the governing provisions accurately is therefore a practical deployment question
as well as a research one.

RAG and its evaluation. Retrieval-augmented generation
conditions a generator on passages retrieved from a corpus [1], with dense passage retrieval [3] and sparse BM25
scoring [4] as the two dominant retriever families [2]. General RAG suites evaluate component capabilities such as
noise robustness and negative rejection [5], and referencefree frameworks estimate faithfulness and answer relevance
[6], but these suites are English-centric and none scores the
correctness of a statutory citation, the unit a compliance
reviewer trusts. Two legal-domain benchmarks move toward
that requirement. LegalBench-RAG scores retrieval against
gold supporting spans so that generation quality cannot mask
evidence-selection errors [7], and Legal RAG Bench evaluates
end-to-end legal RAG with a hierarchical error decomposition
[8]. We report three failure classes accordingly: retrieval failure, where no gold provision reaches the prompt; generation
failure, where the provision is present but the answer asserts
unsupported claims; and citation mismatch, where the answer
names a wrong or non-governing provision.
Hallucination and faithfulness measurement. Hallucination in natural language generation is surveyed along the
intrinsic/extrinsic axis [9]. In summarisation, faithfulness was
operationalised by evaluating whether source text entails summary facts [10], while atomic-fact decomposition enabled
precision scoring per claim [11]. Attribution research further
formalises the requirement that generated statements be supported by cited sources, scoring citation quality separately
from answer correctness [12]. These risks are documented in
deployment: an audit of leading legal research platforms found
answer, source, citation, and completeness failures on every
tool tested [13]. Our citation metric follows this attribution
line but is stricter in one respect: the cited unit is a statutory
article or clause, so a citation to the right instrument but the
wrong article scores zero, matching how a legal reviewer reads
a reference.
Legal-domain language models. Legal NLP benchmarks
such as LexGLUE establish that legal text behaves differently

III. T HE B ENCHMARK
A. Domain and QA-driven corpus construction
The domain is Vietnamese banking and payment regulation:
SBV circulars on payment services, cards, non-cash payment
and credit-institution organisation, together with the governing
decrees and laws. Rather than querying a broad collection of
legal instruments, the retrieval corpus is derived directly from
the benchmark questions. Each gold question was authored
together with a verbatim passage from the instrument that
answers it and the source URL of that instrument. Instrument
identifiers are resolved from the URL through a humanverified manifest; where a URL is opaque, the row is kept
with an explicit UNRESOLVED: identifier and reported, never
dropped, so the benchmark cannot silently shrink. When
a passage cites multiple candidate instruments, the named
instrument is primary; spreadsheet URL ordering provides no
priority.
The current benchmark holds 88 rows: 64 answerable
questions, each with a gold passage recording the supporting
provision and at least one source URL, and 24 unanswerable
probes. Because gold passages reflect annotator records and
may not represent verbatim extracts, the pipeline verifies each
passage against the corpus at ingestion. When a recorded
passage is a summary rather than an exact extract, the verbatim
article text is substituted from the source instrument and
logged. In the delivered revision, 63 of the 64 passages consist
of verbatim article text substituted under a guarded repair
procedure, which rewrites a row only if the cited instrument is
present in the corpus, the cited article exists, and the annotator
summary clears a lexical overlap threshold against that article;

B. Two-tier corpus and the coverage invariant
The corpus has two tiers with identical interfaces. Tier 1
is a development fixture of the verbatim gold passages, one
chunk per passage, built so that indexing, retrieval, prompting,
generation and scoring could be integrated before the real
instruments arrived. Although each question’s gold passage
is in Tier 1 by construction, top-3 retrieval over the 64chunk fixture is not guaranteed to surface it—the gold chunk
reaches the top 3 for only 33 of 64 answerable questions under
BM25 and 47 of 64 under dense retrieval—so Tier 1 measures
generation under a mixture of evidence conditions. Tier 1 is
therefore never treated as corpus evidence, and the generation
campaign that used it runs under the disclosure of §V-A. Tier 2
is the segmented full text of the ingested instruments and is the
corpus on which retrieval quality is measured (Table III). Segmentation follows the statutory hierarchy: articles (Ðiều) are
chunked at clause (Khoan) boundaries—one chunk per clause,
short articles single chunks—and preambles are retained rather
than discarded.
The coverage invariant enforces alignment between the tiers:
every retained answerable question’s verbatim gold passage
must match a Tier 2 chunk exactly under strict normalisation,
in either direction over at least 30 characters—for clausechunked articles the match is the governing clause inside the
passage—with the single annex-citing row of §III-A excepted
(Unicode NFC plus whitespace collapse, diacritics preserved).
A passage matching only after diacritic folding is classified as
extraction damage, since lost diacritics would corrupt retrieval
and citation scoring. A passage matching only after whitespace
removal is logged as a second diagnostic category, an artifact
of gazette PDFs that insert spurious intra-word spaces. Neither
type of degradation is accepted as an exact match. Any
question whose governing instrument could not be obtained
is excluded from the answerable set; retaining it would falsely
penalise models for hallucinating evidence that was never
retrievable.

C. Unanswerable probes
The 24 probes evaluate the abstention component of RQ2
using questions deliberately unanswerable from the corpus;
their gold target is the sentinel string Không có trong kho
văn ban (“not in the document corpus”). These scenarios
represent plausible, in-domain banking situations whose governing provisions lie outside the repository. Examples include
opening payment accounts for condominium management
boards, kindergartens, and foreign trade-representative offices;
collateral held by a spouse or an owner under 18; and the
reporting frequency of the capital-adequacy ratio. The capitaladequacy question was originally annotated as answerable, but
source verification revealed it is governed by an uningested
statistical-reporting circular, prompting its reassignment to the
probe arm.
By testing realistic questions governed by missing instruments, the probes evaluate abstention under a corpus-coverage
gap rather than simple rejection of out-of-domain topics.
This setting is intentionally demanding: abstention cannot be
triggered by topical distance but requires recognising that the
retrieved evidence does not support an answer. We state as
a limitation that the probe set contains no precision-trap and
no cross-jurisdictional arm, so abstention on numerically nearmiss questions and on foreign rules is not measured. A model
that answers a probe fluently has failed abstention even if
the answer is globally true, because the benchmark strictly
evaluates what the corpus supports.

D. Annotation and inter-annotator reliability
Questions were authored by two domain annotators and
verified against their source instruments at ingestion (§III-A);
wrong labels were corrected from documented findings rather
than scored as-is. Inter-annotator agreement on the gold labels
was not measured, and we state the resulting author-bias risk
as a limitation.

A. Retrieval
We compare three retrieval modes at top-k = 3. Under
closed-book, the prompt contains only the question without
context. For RAG-BM25, queries and corpus chunks are segmented with PyVi [22] to preserve compound legal terms,
followed by BM25 scoring [4], [23]. For RAG-Dense, queries
and chunks are embedded using multilingual BGE-M3 [24]
and ranked by cosine similarity. In both RAG configurations,
the top-3 retrieved passages are concatenated into the prompt
with identical formatting under a shared 7,000-character context budget (447 of 528 RAG prompts are clipped and 240
ranked passages dropped), so retrieval representation is the
only factor varied by design, though the budget’s clipping
depends on each retriever’s ranking.
B. Generation
We evaluate three instruction-tuned models of at most 7B
parameters: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct
[25], alongside the Vietnamese-focused Vistral-7B-Chat [26]

as an architecturally distinct third model. All models are served
in 4-bit quantisation (NF4 weights, float16 compute) on a
single 24 GB GPU. Each model passes an automated memory
preflight, and chat-template application is verified on sample
outputs. Every prompt includes explicit instructions specifying
a refusal keyphrase, ensuring abstention is measured as a
deliberate behavior rather than an omission.

V. E XPERIMENTAL D ESIGN AND R ESULTS

For each evaluation instance, we compute four core metrics. Groundedness assesses whether atomic assertions in
the generated answer are entailed by the retrieved context,
following claim-level verification methodologies [11], [12],
and the hallucination rate identifies statements that contradict
statutory text or introduce unsupported assertions. Citation
precision, recall, and F1 are scored at the article (Ðiều) level,
with optional credit for clause (Khoan) accuracy (citations to
the correct article number under an incorrect legal instrument
score zero). Abstention accuracy on unanswerable probes measures whether the model emits the instructed refusal keyphrase
without generating substantive claims.
Our automated scoring pipeline is deterministic, lexical, and
released in full without manual adjustment. We validated it
against 50 expert-annotated responses stratified across models
and modes. Every correctness and abstention call the scorer
makes is confirmed by the expert (3/3 and 10/10), but it misses
15 expert-correct answers and 8 expert-judged abstentions, so
those positive calls are exact and those rates conservative.
Scorer–expert agreement is κ = 0.20 for correctness, 0.39 for
hallucination, and 0.62 for abstention [27]; on hallucination the
scorer misses ten and over-flags five, netting the undercount
of five that makes the rates in Table I mild underestimates.
The validation post-dates the campaign: Tables I and II were
aggregated with the scorer still unvalidated (strict=False
in the log), and the κ values above are the completed check.

A. Evidence Conditioning and Scope
The generation experiments run on the curated Tier-1 fixture, in which every answerable question’s supporting statutory
passage is present by design. The guarantee holds at the pool
level, not the prompt level (§III-B): the results below mix
generation-side citation errors with fixture-retrieval misses,
and RQ2 splits the unfaithfulness rates by evidence condition.
Three implications follow:
• Upper-Bound Conditioning: Tables I and II measure
generation on a fixture whose pool contains every gold
passage; the gains over closed-book baselines are therefore an empirical upper bound on retrieval-augmented
performance.
• Arm Differences: differences between RAG-BM25 and
RAG-Dense reflect both retrieval behaviour on the fixture
and prompt composition; the two configurations yield
identical answers in only 45 of 264 shared instances.
• Independent Retrieval Evaluation: Retrieval performance is evaluated independently on the 3,703-chunk
Tier-2 corpus (Table III) across 22 instruments.

D. RQ3: document-level retrieval is strong, article-level is not
On the Tier-2 corpus (Table III), BM25 with Vietnamese
segmentation retrieves a chunk of the cited instrument for
90.6% of answerable questions at k=3 (73.4% at k=1) but
places the cited Ðiều itself in the top 3 for only 49.2% (38.1%
at k=1). The dense arm is close behind at document level
(89.1% at k=3) and edges BM25 at article level (52.4%
against 49.2%)—a two-question difference, 33 versus 31 of
63, that this sample size cannot resolve. Document-level
retrieval is strong but incomplete, with six (BM25) and seven
(dense) of 64 questions retrieving no chunk of the governing
instrument at k=3, and placing the specific governing article in
context remains difficult: unless a model cites correctly from
parametric memory, only about half of questions can carry the
governing article into the prompt, capping end-to-end citation
well below the conditioned rates of RQ2.
VI. C ONCLUSION
RegRAG-VN establishes an empirical evaluation framework
for regulatory RAG by coupling benchmark queries directly to
statutory provisions, scoring citations at article and clause resolution, and assessing abstention under corpus coverage gaps.
Retrieval augmentation shifts small models from answering
toward refusal, and the raw hallucination reduction survives
that correction only for the smallest model; citation fidelity
remains the critical vulnerability, with 43–49% of RAG answers on answerable questions failing citation-level checking,
a rate near 35% even when the source provision reaches the
prompt intact, pooled clause accuracy at or below 0.32, and
brittle refusal calibration throughout. Retrieval experiments on
the full 3,703-chunk statutory corpus show that documentlevel recall at k=3 reaches 90% while article-level recall drops
below 53%. By formalizing a provenance-guarded evaluation
architecture, we provide a verifiable foundation for future
research on faithful legal language models.
